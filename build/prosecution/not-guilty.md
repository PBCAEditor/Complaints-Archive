> **This guide concerns the existing sections 21 to 25 of the Landlord and Tenant Act 1985.** In England they are being replaced in stages under the Leasehold and Freehold Reform Act 2024. The section 22 and 23 duties, and the offence of breaching them, end on 1 April 2027. The section 21 duty and its offence continue until 1 November 2027 for private landlords and 1 November 2028 for housing associations and other social landlords. After those dates, failures go to the tribunal instead of the criminal courts. Dates for Wales have not yet been set. If the failure you want to prosecute happened before one of these dates, you may still be able to prosecute it within the usual six months, but take advice first.

**Note:** This companion guide should be read alongside our main [Guide to Private Prosecutions under Section 25](/resources/prosecution/), particularly the section explaining which landlords can and cannot be prosecuted in England and Wales.

## The booklet

The booklet is a Word document, built to be printed and brought to the plea hearing. It has the pages below, and a model of the directions you can hand to the court. The first part, on this page, explains what a not-guilty plea changes. The rest of the booklet is a set of worksheets you fill in at court.

[Download the whole booklet (Word)](/resources/prosecution/downloads/s25-not-guilty-booklet.docx)

Inside the booklet, the In-Court Guide and Checklist has these sections:

- **A. Case and plea.** Record basic details and the defence representative.
- **B. What is actually disputed.** The most important part of the hearing.
- **C. What facts and documents can be agreed.** Remove undisputed matters from the trial.
- **D. Further prosecution evidence and outstanding enquiries.** Does anything else need to be served or checked?
- **E. Initial prosecution disclosure.** Unused material.
- **F. Defence statement and defence witness information.** What the defendant must or may provide.
- **G. Prosecution witnesses.** Who needs to attend and what can be read.
- **H. Defence witnesses.** Record what the defendant says about its witnesses.
- **I. Evidential or procedural issues.** Hearsay, experts, witness summonses.
- **J. Points of law or fact.** Complex or unusual issues that may need deciding.
- **K. Other directions.** Anything not covered above.
- **L. Complete timetable.** Every deadline in one place.
- **M. Trial arrangements.** Date, time, venue, estimate.
- **N. Before leaving court.** Final checklist.

## What a not-guilty plea changes

A not-guilty plea does not normally mean that the hearing simply ends and you are sent away with a trial date. The court will usually move straight on to preparing the case for trial. You should be prepared to discuss the case with the defendant's representative and the court's legal adviser immediately after the not-guilty plea has been entered.

You should have immediately to hand:

- This booklet, open at section A
- Your proposed directions (at the back of this booklet)
- Your IDPC
- A notebook and pen to jot down any dates and information discussed

This is not the trial. You are helping the court establish what the trial will be about and what must happen before it.

## Preparing for case management

When the defendant pleads not guilty, the court will usually move straight into case management. You will need two documents. Your **proposed directions** tell the court what you think should happen before trial. Your **in-court guide and checklist** (the sections of the booklet that follow) records what the court actually decides. Both cover the same ground. You offer the first and fill in the second.

The court may use a Preparation for Effective Trial (PET) form to record the information needed to organise the trial and the directions made at the hearing. It covers matters such as what is disputed, further evidence, disclosure, witnesses, deadlines and the trial arrangements. You do not need to work through the full form in court. This booklet is designed to capture the same information in a simpler format, so that you can complete any PET the court requires afterwards.

> **You do not need to prepare a separate PET form in advance unless the court has directed you to do so. If you do not have the information needed to complete the PET form before the plea hearing, make sure you leave with the information needed to complete one.**

Have at least three copies of your proposed directions to hand. If the court asks what directions the prosecution proposes, use them as your prompt. If the legal adviser or magistrates want them in writing, hand them a copy. The court may adopt them, modify them, or work through the issues in its own way. Do not insist on your proposed directions being followed exactly.

The court may not deal with sections A to N in the order they appear in the booklet. The legal adviser may deal with matters in a different order. Tick and write down the answers as they arise.
