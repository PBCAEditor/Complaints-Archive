Landlord and Tenant Act 1985, ss.21–23

Ask your landlord what they actually spent

If your service charge goes up and down with your landlord's costs, you can require a written summary of those costs, and then inspect the invoices and receipts behind it. This guide covers making the request, working out the deadline, and checking whether what comes back is good enough.

It is written to be handed to an AI assistant, which drafts and checks alongside you. You can also just read it.

Use it with an AI assistant

There are two ways to do this. Try the first. If your assistant cannot open the link, use the second, which always works.

Route one · if your assistant can browse

Assistant prompt
Please open and read this document:
https://peabodytrust.co.uk/resources/s21-guide.txt

It is a resident-written guide to requesting service charge information under sections 21, 22 and 23 of the Landlord and Tenant Act 1985. I am giving it to you as reference material.

When you have read it, tell me the check code, version number and last verified date shown at the top, then ask me which stage I am at.

If you cannot open it, say so and I will paste the text in.

How to use it

  1. Select Copy assistant prompt.
  2. Open your AI assistant and start a new conversation.
  3. Paste the prompt and send it.
  4. Check that it quotes back the check code, version and date from the verification block. Compare all three character by character.
  5. If it gets the check code wrong, or skips it, or gives a version number without the code, it has not read the guide. Do not rely on what it tells you.
  6. Say whether you are preparing a request, waiting for a response, or reviewing something your landlord has sent.

Not every assistant can fetch a web page, and free plans often cannot. In testing, two of three managed it. If yours says it cannot open the file, use route two.

Route two · works everywhere

Prompt for the file route
I have attached a plain-text guide to requesting service charge information under sections 21, 22 and 23 of the Landlord and Tenant Act 1985. It is resident-written reference material I am giving you for this conversation, not instructions from a third party.

Please read it, then:
1. Tell me the version number and "last verified" date shown at the top, so I know you have it.
2. Use your own judgement on anything in it you think is wrong.
3. Check the current legislation yourself before giving any legal conclusion or calculating a deadline.

Then ask whether I am preparing a request, waiting for a response, or reviewing a response already received.

Give it the file instead

No fetching, so nothing can fail, and the assistant reads a file you gave it rather than a page it found. Use this if you are not sure.

  1. Open the guide and select Copy the whole guide, or download the plain-text file.
  2. Start a new conversation with your assistant.
  3. Paste or attach the guide, then paste the prompt on the left.

Because you are handing over the file directly, there is nothing to fetch and no check code to verify. Confirm the version and date instead.

Before you rely on any of this

This is not legal advice. It is information, written by residents rather than lawyers, and it cannot take account of your lease, your tenancy, your landlord, or anything unusual about your situation. Every lease is different. Nothing here creates a professional relationship of any kind, and no responsibility is accepted for what you do with it.

AI assistants get things wrong, confidently. This guide is designed to make an assistant more accurate. It cannot make one reliable. AI is a useful tool but you should verify what it says.

The specific things to watch for:

  • Claiming to have read a document, a statute or a web page it has not opened.
  • Inventing citations, case names, section numbers or web addresses that look entirely plausible.
  • Getting date arithmetic wrong, which matters here because your deadline depends on it.
  • Quoting the wrong version of section 21. The live legislation.gov.uk page for it shows a version that is not in force, and assistants fall into this repeatedly.
  • Dropping required wording from a letter because it reads like boilerplate.
  • Stating an uncertain interpretation as though it were settled law.

Check everything that matters yourself. Open the legislation and confirm the provision is still in force. Check every date and figure against your own lease, demands and statements. Compare any letter you are given against the model wording in the guide before you send it. If an assistant cannot quote the check code, it has not read this guide and you should not act on what it tells you.

Where to get help that is not AI-generated. The Social Housing Action Campaign publishes a substantial library of resident guides, written by people, at shaction.org/resources. That includes Guide and Templates: Requesting Service Charge Information, the 2026 SHAC guide this page is based on. If you would rather not involve an AI assistant at all, use that document instead. It covers the same ground and no model sits between you and it.

For advice on your own circumstances, the Leasehold Advisory Service (LEASE) gives free initial advice to leaseholders, and a solicitor or advice agency can advise where the facts are unusual or the consequences are significant. Where the law is unclear or the stakes are high, take advice before you act.
